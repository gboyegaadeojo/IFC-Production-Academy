# Camera Operations curriculum

Draft for the pilot (decision 5 in [2026-10-31 open decisions](../decisions/2026-10-31-open-decisions.md)).
G and the Camera lead check every item against IFC's cameras, positions, and director calls by
**15 Nov 2026**, then enter it in the Academy, where it's published and versioned. After that, the
Academy is the source of truth and this file is the starting draft.

Quiz questions and answers are **not** in this repository, because it's public. They're kept in

Wherever this says "IFC's setting", the Camera lead fills in the actual value in the camera SOP.

  Gimbal, Camera Lead and Trainer. Expiry off.

  Fundamentals, Live Shot Operation); Roaming Camera Operator (Live Shot Operation, Handheld and
  Gimbal).
- **Level prerequisites:** Level 2 needs the Level 1 course completed and Camera Fundamentals
  approved. Level 3 needs the same for Level 2.

## Level 1: Foundations

**Outcome:** you set up and shut down a camera position safely, frame clean basic shots, follow
the director's calls, and serve the wide camera on your own.
**Time:** about 2 hours of lessons, then one service beside a trained operator, and the practical.

Each lesson has a 3 to 8 minute video filmed at IFC (see "Filming" under each lesson), written key
points that stand in until the video exists, and the SOPs it relies on.

### Module A: Your role

**1. The camera operator's role in a service** (4 min)
- Objectives: explain who the cameras serve (the room on the screens, people watching online,
  the archive); name the camera positions at IFC and what each one is for.
- Key points: the camera team helps people engage with worship and the Word, without drawing
  attention to itself. The director chooses what goes to air; the operator offers clean, steady
  options. Online viewers see *only* what the cameras give them.
- Filming: a walk-through of each camera position during a rehearsal, with the director's view on
  the multiview.

**2. Team communication and service etiquette** (6 min)
- Objectives: use the headset and talkback correctly; answer with your camera number; know when
  to stay silent.
- Key points: say your camera number first ("Two: I have a tight of the keys"). Keep the button
  down only while speaking. Stay silent in prayer, Scripture reading, and quiet moments unless
  something is wrong. Arrive at IFC's call time, phone silenced and away. Dress in dark clothes so
  you disappear in the room.
- Ministry moments: don't close in on people in the congregation during prayer, response, or
  ministry time unless the director asks. Never frame a child up close (IFC's photo policy).
- Filming: a short scene of good and poor talkback, recorded from the director's desk.
- SOP: Camera comms and etiquette.

### Module B: The camera

**3. Camera system orientation and equipment handling** (8 min)
- Objectives: name the camera, lens, tripod, and head controls at IFC; carry and adjust
  equipment safely.
- Key points: the camera body, lens (zoom and focus controls, servo zoom switch), viewfinder or
  monitor, tally light, tripod legs and spreader, the head's pan and tilt locks and drag, the
  quick-release plate and its lock. Always lock pan and tilt before you let go. Never leave a camera
  on an unlocked head. Tape down cables across walkways.
- Filming: close-ups of every control on IFC's actual camera and tripod, labelled on screen.
- SOP: Camera position setup.

**4. Framing and composition** (7 min)
- Objectives: frame using the rule of thirds, headroom, and look room; avoid cutting at joints.
- Key points: eyes sit on the top third line. Headroom is a small gap above the head: too much
  and the shot looks empty, too little and it feels cramped. Leave look room on the side the person
  faces. Don't crop at the neck, waist, knees, or ankles; crop between joints. Keep the horizon and
  the stage edges level.
- Filming: the same speaker framed well and badly, side by side; IFC service footage for good
  examples.

**5. Focus and exposure fundamentals** (8 min)
- Objectives: set focus reliably; set exposure to IFC's settings and check it on a face.
- Key points:
  - **Focus:** zoom all the way in on the speaker's eyes, set focus, then zoom back out to your
    shot. This works because IFC's lens holds focus through the zoom (the Camera lead confirms it
    does). Recheck whenever the speaker moves forward or back. Use focus peaking or magnify if
    the camera has them.
  - **Exposure:** set iris, gain, and shutter to IFC's settings. In Canada mains power is 60 Hz,
    so keep the shutter at 1/60 (or IFC's setting) to avoid flicker from the stage lights. Use
    zebras to check faces: a few stripes on the brightest part of the face at IFC's zebra level is
    right. Stripes across the whole face means it's too bright.
  - **White balance:** use IFC's preset, or set it on a white card under stage light when the
    director asks, so every camera matches.
- Filming: a screen recording of the viewfinder while focus is set; the zebras on a face, too
  bright and correct.
- SOP: Camera settings (IFC's values).

**6. Shot types and when to use them** (5 min)
- Objectives: frame the five standard shots on request.
- Key points: **Wide** (the whole stage, the safe shot); **Medium** (waist up); **Medium
  close-up** (chest up, the usual speaker shot); **Close-up** (head and shoulders); **Two-shot**
  (two people, for an interview or a duet). Know which shot your position is responsible for.
- Filming: each shot from IFC's camera positions, labelled.

**7. Camera movement and stability** (6 min)
- Objectives: make smooth pans, tilts, and zooms; hold a steady frame.
- Key points: set the drag so the head moves smoothly, not loosely. Ease in, move at a steady
  speed, ease out. Move for a reason. Don't pan and zoom at the same time unless the director asks.
  When you're live (red tally), hold still. If the speaker drifts, make only small, slow
  corrections.
- Filming: the same pan done smoothly and jerkily; the drag settings.

### Module C: In the service

**8. Following direction from the director** (7 min)
- Objectives: respond to the standard calls and the tally light.
- Key points:
  - **Tally:** red means you're live on the program feed: hold your shot. Green, where IFC uses
    it, means preview: you're next, so finish your framing now.
  - **Calls:** "Ready two" or "Standby two" means you're about to go live: settle your shot.
    "Take two" means you're live. "Two, you're clear" means you're off air, so you're free to
    reframe. "Two, tighten up" or "loosen" means change the shot size. "Two, find the drummer"
    means find that shot and say when you have it.
  - Answer when asked ("Two, ready"). If you can't get a shot, say so right away rather than
    staying quiet.
- Filming: the director's view and a camera's view side by side, through one song.
- SOP: Director calls (IFC's version).

**9. Setup, shutdown, and equipment care** (6 min)
- Objectives: set up and shut down your position by the SOP.
- Key points:
  - **Setup:** legs spread and locked, head level (bubble centred), camera locked on the plate,
    power and signal on, tally check, comms check, settings (lesson 5), focus check on the
    speaker's mark, and white balance when called.
  - **Shutdown:** lock pan and tilt, lens cap on, power down in IFC's order, coil cables over
    and under (never round your elbow), batteries on charge, and the position left tidy.
- Filming: the full setup at IFC's main camera position, at real speed.
- SOP: Camera position setup; Camera shutdown.

**10. Common problems and when to escalate** (5 min)
- Objectives: recognise common faults; know what to fix yourself and what to call in.
- Key points:
  - **Fix yourself:** soft focus (refocus), poor framing (reframe when clear), loose drag (adjust
    when clear).
  - **Call the director straight away:** no picture or signal, no tally, a warning on the camera,
    a battery under 20%, or someone standing in your shot's path.
  - **If comms fail:** use IFC's hand signal to the director's position.
  - **Never** power-cycle a camera that's live; ask first. Safety comes first: if a cable or
    tripod is a trip hazard, fix it or call it in.
- Filming: short scenarios with the right response.

### Module D: Readiness

**11. Practical demonstration and evaluator feedback** (4 min)
- Objectives: know exactly what the practical checks and how it's decided.
- Key points: a trainer observes you through a real service or rehearsal using the checklist
  below, and ticks what they see. The Academy works out the result from the checklist. The
  trainer doesn't choose the outcome. "Not yet" isn't a failure, and it never stops you serving;
  it tells you what to practise, and you try again whenever you're ready.
- Filming: a trainer walking through the checklist with a volunteer.

**12. Final readiness review** (3 min)
- Objectives: check yourself against the checklist before you request your assessment.
- Key points: serve at least one service beside a trained operator.
  Review the setup SOP the night before. Then tap **Request assessment**.
- Quiz: the Level 1 quiz (15 questions; 80% to pass; 3 attempts; answers shown once you pass or
  use every attempt). It must be passed before the practical.

## Level 2: Live Shots

**Outcome:** you serve a follow camera on your own: tracking movement, keeping focus, and
anticipating shots in worship and preaching.

Lessons (outline; the Camera lead writes them after the pilot):

1. Following a moving speaker: lead room on the move, and smooth, anticipatory pans
2. Holding focus on movement: following focus, and when to loosen the shot instead
3. Worship coverage: knowing the song structure, solos and instrumental shots, the band
4. Anticipation: listening to the music and the speaker; offering the next shot before it's needed
5. Matching other cameras: shot sizes, angles, and not duplicating another camera's shot
6. Scripture, slides, and lower thirds: leaving space for graphics
7. Special services: baptisms, dedications, guests (and IFC's photo policy for children)
8. Recovering on air: what to do when a shot goes wrong while you're live

**Quiz:** scenario questions; 80% to pass; 3 attempts.

## Level 3: Advanced and Leading

**Outcome:** you operate handheld and gimbal cameras safely, and you train and assess others.

Lessons (outline): handheld technique and stance; gimbal balancing and modes; moving safely
through the room and on the stage; working with the lighting team; creative shots and when not to
take them; teaching a new operator; giving feedback; assessing with the checklist and calibrating
with another trainer.

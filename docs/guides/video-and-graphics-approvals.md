# Video and graphics approvals

Everything shown during a livestream is checked for copyright first. This replaces the old JotForm
"Video / Graphics Approval Form". It's switched on by an admin once Quality control and the emails
are set up; until then the form says requests are paused.

## Asking to show a video or graphic

Anyone can ask, with no account, at **/media-request** (for example
`https://your-academy-address/media-request`). Share that link where the JotForm link used to be.

1. Enter your name and email. The answer comes to that email.
2. Choose **A video** or **A graphic**.
   - A video is always a link: YouTube, Vimeo or Google Drive. Make sure anyone with the link can
     view it.
   - A graphic can be uploaded (PNG, JPEG, WebP or PDF, up to 20 MB) or linked.
3. Choose the date it should be shown. If there's a service that day in Planning Center, the
   request is linked to it.
4. Say what it's for, such as "a missions update before the offering", and press **Send request**.

**The deadline.** Requests need to arrive a set number of days before the date: 5 days unless an
admin changes it, counted as calendar days or business days (Monday to Friday). The form says which
date is the earliest you can choose, and the date picker starts there. If you choose an earlier
date, the form says it's too close to check in time and won't send it: choose a later date, or ask
the production team directly. (An admin can choose to accept late requests instead, marked Late.)

You can send up to 5 requests a day from one email address.

## Checking on your request

Within a couple of minutes you get a "We got your request" email with a **See its progress** link. That page
shows:

- **Sent**, with the time.
- **With Quality control**, who look at every request first.
- **Copyright review, if needed.** Only some requests need it.
- **Decision**, approved or not, with any note from the team.

Only people with your email's link can see it. Keep the email: the link is the only way back.

## The answer

You get an email when it's decided: **Approved**, or **Not approved** with what would help. If it
wasn't approved, the email and the progress page have a **Send a new version** button.

## For Quality control

Quality control doesn't need an Academy account. Each new request is emailed to everyone on the
Quality control list with a **Review it** button. That link is just for you: it opens your list of
open requests and works for 30 days, as long as you're on the list. Each new email brings a fresh
link.

1. Open a request. Your name goes on it, so two people don't check the same one.
2. Watch the video or open the graphic, and tick the checks if they help. They never decide for
   you.
3. Then choose what happens next:
   - **Approve** or **Not approved**, with a note. The requester is emailed straight away. If you
     decline, say what would help.
   - **Copyright review**, if the rights need a closer look. It goes to every copyright reviewer,
     in the Academy and outside it. Write what they should check, such as "Is the background song
     covered by our streaming licence?"

Requests decided in the last 30 days stay on your list underneath.

## For copyright reviewers

Quality control only sends a request for a copyright review when it needs one. The first reviewer
to decide closes it. The decision goes straight to the requester, and Quality control gets a copy.

- **In the Academy.** If you have the Copyright reviewer role, **Copyright** appears in your side
  bar on a computer or tablet, with a count of what's waiting. On a phone, tap **Serve** on the
  bottom bar, then **Copyright**; a number on **Serve** means reviews are waiting. You also get a notification in the
  bell, and an alert on your phone if alerts are on (choose under **Copyright review** in your
  notification settings). The email's **Review it** button opens the same request, after you sign
  in.
- **Outside the Academy.** You don't need an account. The email's **Review it** link opens that one
  request. It stops working once someone decides, or after 14 days.

Either way, you see Quality control's question, the video or graphic, and the details. Choose
**Approve** or **Not approved** and write a note for the requester. Need to ask something first?
Reply to the email.

## Cleared to show (everyone on duty)

Once something is approved, everyone scheduled on that service in Planning Center sees it, whatever
their position. So if someone covers Computer Graphics, they see it too.

- **On your Training page:** a **Cleared to show** card for each service you're on, from the moment
  something is approved until the service ends.
- **On that day's checklist:** **Cleared to show today**, in order of service. Each item says where
  it plays, such as "at Offering", or "Not placed yet".

Each item has **Open video** or **Download**, any note from the reviewer, and a **Loaded** tick.
The tick is shared, so the producer and everyone else on duty can see what's ready.

**Only show what's on the list.** If someone hands you a video or graphic that isn't there, it
hasn't been cleared. Ask the producer before it goes on screen.

The media inbox also gets a **Cleared to show** email for each approval. Its subject always starts
"Approved media:", so a Gmail label can sort them.

## Setting it up (admins)

**Admin → Video and graphics approvals** is for Platform Administrators. Every change is in the
audit log.

1. **Quality control.** Add each person's name and email. Nobody needs an Academy account. Removing
   someone stops their links straight away.
2. **Copyright reviewers.** Academy members get the **Copyright Reviewer** role in **People and
   roles**, which puts Copyright in their side bar. Add people outside the Academy by name and email.
3. **The form, the deadline, and what happens on approval.**
   - The deadline in days, counted as calendar or business days, and whether a late request is
     refused (the default) or accepted, marked Late.
   - An optional introduction for the form. Write {deadline} where the deadline should go.
   - The checks reviewers tick, one per line. They help people decide; they don't decide for them.
   - The media inbox, which gets a "Cleared to show" email for each approval, and whether everyone
     on duty that day gets one too.
4. **Emails.** Six emails, each with a subject and message. Press a fill-in, such as
   {first name} or {service date}, to add it where your cursor is. The preview uses an example
   request. **Save** keeps your version, **Send me a test** emails you the saved version within a
   couple of minutes (even while approvals are off), and **Put back the original** undoes your changes. Keep
   "Approved media:" at the start of the Cleared to show subject if a Gmail label sorts them.
5. **Turn it on** in **Admin → Features**: *Video and graphics approvals*. Then put the
   /media-request link where the old JotForm link was.

**All requests** lists every request, newest service first. Open one to see its details and who it
was sent to.

## Words we use

| Word            | Meaning                                                                       |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Quality control | The team that checks every request first                                      |
| Copyright review | A closer look at the rights, only when Quality control asks for one          |
| Late            | Sent with less notice than the deadline                                       |
| Reference       | The request's number, such as CR-0142, to quote if you have a question        |
